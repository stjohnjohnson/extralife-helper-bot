# Campaign progress and sequential celebrations

Status: experience agreed in conversation; implementation plan presented for review. This revision supersedes the broadcast-total and simultaneous-party requirements in issue #67 and earlier Stream Avatars plans. The existing implementation remains unchanged until plan review.

## Experience

Every newly detected donation first receives its thank-you: amount caption, following hearts, and the existing random jump/hug action. This includes matching donations, which the operator confirmed arrive as ordinary donation records. The donation's size does not determine the party; crossing a configured cumulative campaign threshold does.

The thank-you lasts five seconds. Nearby gifts combine their amounts/counts and can extend it to ten seconds from its original start. After the thank-you finishes, a qualifying campaign milestone receives the existing 18-second confetti/dance party. Reaching the participant's fundraising goal receives the distinct 20-second goal party instead of an additional milestone party for the same crossing. A batch crossing several thresholds marks all handled and plays one party for the highest crossing.

Use the participant campaign's actual total, including fundraising before this stream and matching. With a $500 interval, $2,475 plus a $50 donation becomes a $50 thank-you followed by `$2,525 RAISED!`. Retain whole-dollar formatting without `.00`; nonzero cents remain. The goal caption remains `EXTRA LIFE GOAL REACHED!`. Hidden donation amounts retain generic `THANK YOU!`; they do not make the authoritative campaign total incomplete.

New gifts received during an active party wait in one combined pending batch. Finish the current party, show that batch's thank-you, then its highest applicable party. Do not interrupt a party or build a per-gift queue. This is a safeguard: ordinary five-second recognition plus an 18/20-second party takes 23/25 seconds, and extended recognition takes up to 28/30 seconds. The existing 30-second poll usually keeps them apart, but request delays and delayed goal confirmation can overlap.

Donation and campaign responses arrive separately under the existing API pacing. Start recognition promptly; start a party only after both its thank-you and a fresh authoritative total are available. A delayed total may leave a gap after recognition. Do not estimate a campaign total by adding gifts to a stale total, block thanks behind the campaign request, or show a second thank-you when the total finally confirms the crossing.

## Progress and lifecycle

Celebration eligibility has no Twitch online/offline dependency, broadcast start/end window, interruption grace, elapsed-stream counter or recovery requirement based on being offline. Stream Avatars readiness determines whether there is somewhere to display an effect. Twitch viewer monitoring, stream markers and their own online requirements continue independently.

Maintain participant-bound campaign progress in exact integer cents. Use fresh `sumDonations` and `fundraisingGoal` observations from the existing participant API. Remember seen donation IDs, the greatest handled campaign threshold, the current goal and whether its goal party has been handled. Do not maintain a separate stream-money ledger or sum the year's donations to obtain the campaign total.

Startup/restart first establishes a silent donation and campaign baseline. Existing gifts, already crossed milestones and an already-met goal never animate. A missing first campaign response leaves milestone detection uninitialized, while fresh valid gifts can still receive thanks. The first successful total establishes the baseline silently. A total change needs a recently accepted fresh donation (within the existing 120-second confirmation window) to generate a party; old amount revelations and unexplained historical corrections advance accounting silently. Matching uses the same path as any other fresh donation.

Repeated/stale observations and repeated IDs do nothing. Monetary decreases do not rearm milestones or the goal; future genuine new thresholds can still celebrate. Changing the interval silently rebases threshold progress. Changing the goal silently establishes its new comparison baseline, rather than manufacturing a crossing; once the campaign's goal celebration is handled, a goal edit does not rearm it. A different participant starts independent campaign progress.

Account and mark thresholds handled even while the companion is unavailable. Drop display intents while disconnected; reconnect shows current progress but does not replay old thanks, pending batches or parties. Correlation, pending visual state and active timers are transient and discarded on restart, disconnect, explicit reset/recovery and shutdown.

Use a new participant-validated `campaign.json` beside the existing `state.json`, leaving legacy broadcast files intact for rollback. Never reinterpret old `liveTotalCents` as campaign money. Reuse the existing atomic write, single-owner lock, backup, quarantine and recovery guarantees through a campaign store with its own schema. Corrupt campaign state disables effects until explicit admin recovery/reset; ordinary bot notifications remain operational. Campaign recovery/reset requires the existing admin authorization and explicit confirmation, with no offline check, and is followed by silent rebasing.

## Architecture and intake

Add a campaign state/reducer/store/controller under `src/streamAvatars/`. Serialize state mutations and persist seen IDs/threshold decisions before displaying an effect. Fence late requests with an intake epoch that changes on startup, reset and recovery, independently of Twitch stream IDs. A temporary development instance uses a separate removable directory and synthetic participant.

Keep the 30-second donation schedule, version 1.4 API contract, descending order, strict timestamp/amount/participant validation, cancellation, request pacing and newest-page notifications. Use the newest page for normal discovery and bounded pagination for exceptional catch-up. A monitoring baseline is a donation-poll checkpoint, not a stream window. It identifies pre-startup history and makes catch-up/deduplication possible; it never supplies campaign money.

For source discovery, retain raw-ID census/count metadata independently of display eligibility. Read a full page even when it contains known IDs. If the record count rises, continue as needed until the increase is explained by new IDs or the pre-baseline boundary is reached; a known first-page ID alone must not hide a later backdated record. Without count metadata, fall back to the existing bounded scan since the monitoring baseline. Do not scan the full year on every poll. Baseline old history silently, diagnose malformed/conflicting rows, and never mark an incomplete page traversal complete. Test first-page and multi-page new gifts, delayed/out-of-order records, changing counts, duplicate pages and cancellation.

The app sends newly normalized gifts to the campaign controller independently of the later total refresh, retaining existing individual Discord/Twitch announcements, stream markers, summaries, goal commands and Hue effects. Initial baselines, updates and in-flight epoch identity are explicit; a cached campaign observation from a donation poll cannot trigger a party as though freshly fetched.

The composer owns the ordered visual state machine: idle, recognition, waiting for confirmation, party, and one pending combined recognition batch. Each accepted polling batch has an opaque ID for correlating delayed total confirmation with already-thanked gifts. Keep correlation only for active/pending/recently thanked batches inside the 120-second window. Reject stale epochs, and never revive a batch after clear/disconnect. One scheduled timer advances phase boundaries; inject the clock/timer interface for tests. Network sends occur when a phase actually starts, with fresh message admission expiry, rather than sending a future party that an old companion would render immediately.

A milestone total update for the same active party does not restart it or dance requests. A late goal confirmation for that same batch upgrades its caption/party to goal, retaining the existing 38-second maximum party span and per-avatar action attempts. Fresh gifts received during a party belong to the pending next batch and cannot be acknowledged or celebrated by upgrading the current one.

## Protocol and companion

Use protocol version 3 because public snapshots lose broadcast-session fields and celebration totals change meaning. Replace `session`/`elapsedMs` with a bounded public campaign snapshot; replace effect `sessionId` with participant campaign identity; replace `liveTotalCents` with `campaignTotalCents`. Carry `sequenceId` and explicit `phase: recognition|party` on celebration messages. Sequence IDs fence action admission; event IDs still deduplicate packets. No donor names, raw donation IDs or arbitrary command/caption strings cross the bridge.

Retain fixed numeric recognition/party fields, strict exact-key validation, 10-second message admission lifetime, generation/mode/identity fencing, JSON-null compatibility and current queue/backpressure limits. Recognition messages carry their five-to-ten-second window and no active party. Party messages carry no donation caption/hearts/action and have an 18/20-second lifetime; same-sequence goal upgrades remain bounded to 38 seconds from party start. Feature/capability lists describe campaign progress rather than broadcast sessions. Reject old companions with an explicit import-latest-v3 diagnostic.

The companion renders one active phase. Recognition is visible before a party, including on milestone/goal gifts. At the transition clear only owned recognition visuals, start confetti/banner/dancing and retain native action ownership rules. Sequence IDs preserve once-per-window jump/hug and once-per-party dance attempts through updates. Keep current per-user real-time dance/hug guards across clears, reconnects and reloads; preserve busy states, random pairing, safe internal targets, native hug completion and gear/decorations. A late eligible avatar may participate once in the active phase. Native access/manual-command limitations remain documented.

Regenerate `sa_raised` from ` RAISED THIS STREAM!` to ` RAISED!`; derive its dimensions from the manifest in Lua/package checks. Keep all 22 assets and the native ZIP format, placeholder credentials and portable packaging.

## Controls, development and validation

`!sa status` reports campaign total/goal, next threshold and companion/state health, with no elapsed-hours or raised-this-stream wording. Replace session reset/recover controls with `campaign reset confirm` and `campaign recover confirm`; both silently rebase after successful state repair. Retire the bridge's offline-grace configuration. Shared Twitch observation validation must move to a neutral helper before deleting unused broadcast modules; viewer logging itself is preserved.

Keep `npm run sa:integration`, its six scenarios, optional physical Hue, `--recognition random|jump|hug`, crowd 0–100, readiness timeout, port-conflict behavior and cleanup. Scenario baselines use campaign totals before synthetic donations. Ordinary/anonymous/batch exercise thanks only; milestone/large exercise thanks then one campaign party; goal exercises thanks then the distinct goal party. The runner waits for both phases and logs their expected order, captions and durations. It loads no real Twitch/Discord/Extra Life/marker clients and touches no production state.

Required coverage includes campaign persistence/restart/legacy isolation, threshold and goal edges, polling/API lag, combined pending gifts, timer cancellation, delayed goal upgrades, old transport rejection, native Lua nulls/actions/cooldowns, resized caption bounds, the 101-avatar budget and unchanged ordinary notifications/Hue. Run full lint/tests and Lua 5.2 companion contracts, rebuild and validate the ZIP, update PR #72 and check required CI. Real-host acceptance must show the new ordering, campaign banner, matching-equivalent donation, goal party and no repeated UserCD warnings. Windows/LAN/OBS capture and optional physical Hue remain deployment checks, never claimed from unit tests.
